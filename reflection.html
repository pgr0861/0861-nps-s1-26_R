A short video and written reflection on the project, teamwork, and my overall learning in the unit.

The project

Our group, Viridus Machina, investigated whether algae grown in photobioreactors could help Swinburne capture its own carbon on campus instead of relying on carbon offsets. My contribution was the data and analysis: my solar exposure spreadsheet showed that from late April to mid-August, Melbourne’s light is too low for Chlorella vulgaris without LED lighting. I also edited our video and wrote parts of the script. I’m happy with the final result we produced.

Teamwork

At the Week 6/7 check-in, our group was on track, but meeting attendance and communication had slipped from our group agreement. After that, we split tasks more clearly and played to each person’s strengths, and our communication improved. The workload wasn’t always even, though. I learned that communicating early saves problems later and that clear roles keep a group on track. Next time, I’d revisit our agreement during the project and speak up sooner about workload. There’s more on this in Task 5.

Overall learning

  • Research and referencing: writing my research paper and bringing its Turnitin similarity score down from 4.0% to 1.0% (Task 1).
  • Where to focus effort: my sustainability challenge showed me that the easiest thing to measure isn’t always the thing that matters most (Task 3).
  • Working with data: using lookup functions to analyse a large dataset for our project (Task 4).
  • Questioning numbers: the guest speakers left me with a healthy scepticism towards “clean energy” claims, and showed me careers that blend science with communication or data (Task 9).
  • Indigenous knowledges: realising how much I didn’t know, and that Indigenous knowledges are grounded in science (Task 7).

Future plans

I want to go into astrophysics research, so I’ll keep building my programming for data analysis and my research and academic writing, and start building a professional network and looking for relevant experience (Task 6). Outside study, I’ll keep switching my devices off at the wall, work with my household on bigger changes like food, and keep learning about Aboriginal astronomy alongside my astrophysics.

Reflection

SSituation

NPS10003 was a core unit in my first year of astrophysics, so I went in with an open mind, hoping to pick up practical skills and some real group project experience.

TTask

Across the semester, I completed the portfolio tasks, from research and spreadsheets to sustainability and cultural competency, while working with my group on our algae project.

AAction

I built my skills through the individual tasks, like my research paper, my sustainability challenge and my project spreadsheet, and contributed data analysis, video editing and scriptwriting to our group project. The hardest parts were keeping group communication on track mid-semester and getting new habits to stick.

RResult

My digital skills self-ratings went up in 11 of 16 areas, my research paper finished with a 1.0% similarity score, and our group produced a final result I’m happy with. The biggest lesson of the unit was that the easiest thing to measure isn’t always the thing that matters most.

What I’d do differently / next

Next time, I’d speak up sooner in group work, lean more on academic sources in my research, and focus my sustainability efforts on the parts of my footprint that matter most, starting with food.
